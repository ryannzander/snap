Does any of this predict winning?tested on 2023, 2024 and 2025

Devpost marks past winners, so this model was run over three finished Hack the Norths — 706 projects with real write-ups, 127 of which won something and 35 of which became Finalists — and scored on whether it ranks them above everyone else. 0.50 is a coin flip. Nobody publishes who advanced to round 2, so the 12 Finalists a year are the closest proxy for surviving the main judging.

There are two scores on this page and they are not equally good. The rating out of 100 is a hand-built measure of the write-up, and it lands near 0.61 at predicting anything. The Finalist model % is a regularised logistic regression fitted on the three past years and validated by holding each year out in turn: 0.744, 95% CI [0.67, 0.81]. It is the column to read. Its output is a calibrated probability, so 10% really does mean about twice the 4.9% base rate. Likes and comments were excluded because they accrue after results are announced — using them scores a fraudulent 0.909. Demo video and gallery images were excluded too: strong historically, but near-empty in a live gallery before the deadline.

Concretely, for Finalists. 5.0% of rated projects became one. Of our top 10, 20% did. Of our #11–25, 2%. Of #26–50, 5%. Of #51–100, 6%. Of #101 and below, 4%. Outside a small head the ranking is flat — it carries no information at all. A third of Finalists sat in our bottom half, and one we ranked 237th.

What winners actually did differently, pooled across the three years: demo video 67% vs 41%, repo linked 94% vs 85%, write-up 617 vs 513 words, tags 4 vs 3. Measured numbers and evidence phrases: no difference at all. Winners also polish their pages after winning, which flatters every number above.
